# Partner network

**Ten kinds of partner.** Shops, brands, testing laboratories, creators and coaches, publishers
and media, syndication partners, data customers, gyms and clubs, platform integrators, research
and charity. Each declares what it gives, what it gets, and the one line that keeps it honest —
labs are paid per assay and never per finding; research partners are allowed to publish findings
that embarrass us.

**Four tiers.** Listed (a working feed) → Verified (registry, VAT, support response) → Partner
(Pro plan plus a measured delivery record) → Strategic (data agreement plus co-operation on one
open data gap a year). No tier buys a ranking position, a label or an editorial mention; the top
tier buys co-operation on data gaps, which is the only thing we actually want from a partner.

**Pipeline** of seven stages, each with an SLA and an explicit exit condition, from Applied
(1 working day to first response) to Partner review (quarterly).

**Five processes**, each with steps, an SLA and a named owner: onboarding (8 working days),
data dispute (24 h first response), delivery claim (5 working days), offboarding (24 h to
suppression — reviews and history stay published, because they belong to the record and not to
the subscription), co-op marketing.

## Affiliate plumbing

Six networks — Direct, Awin, Tradedoubler, Impact, Daisycon, Partnerize — each with its markets,
cookie window, validation delay, payment terms, deduplication policy and our integration method.
Commission is published per category with volume tiers (protein 6→8.5 %, pre-workout 9→12 %,
gainers 4.5→5.5 %).

Six rules keep commission out of the ranking, and the load-bearing ones are:

* Our redirect log is the record. When a network and our log disagree we publish both figures.
* A click we cannot attribute is billed to nobody and reported as unattributed.
* Where two shops offer the same total, the one paying us more does not win — the tie-break is
  measured delivery, then review weight.
* A shop can leave a network and stay listed. Listing is not conditional on a commercial
  relationship, which is the whole argument for trusting the table.
